# Plan 9 (client and site records with an access checklist): open items after the build

Branch `feat/agency-os-clients`, built 2026-11-20 by subagents (all on Sonnet). One migration, `0058_sites` (the `sites` and `site_access_items` tables). Nothing is deployed, pushed, merged or migrated remotely.

What was built: a client is now a place in the app. A client is a business that was won or has a project (no new organization table): `/clients` lists them by name with their Care plan, monthly amount, site count, missing access, what they owe, any billing hold and the next renewal, and a client page (`/clients/:id`) shows the whole picture in one place: people, sites, subscriptions, projects, invoices, proposals and agreements, change orders and the timeline. Each client has one `primary` site, made for you when a sale starts its onboarding or its project (from the lead's website; one per business however many times the payment event repeats), and you can add or archive others by hand. A site carries its address, platform, host, registrar, DNS provider, admin address, who hosts it (Logan, the client, unknown), how you get in (SSH with WP-CLI, MainWP, none, unknown) and a note. Every site has six fixed access items (hosting, DNS, registrar, site admin, Google Business Profile, analytics), each `needed`, `requested`, `received`, `verified` or `not_applicable`, with an optional NAME of the password manager item and a short note. No secret is ever stored: the form says so, and the server refuses names and notes that look like a login or a key. Two flags sit on a site: access still missing, and "Care on a client-hosted site needs SSH with WP-CLI or MainWP". Today has a new `access_missing` row: a client with a live project that started 3 or more days ago and a live site with items not yet in, naming the missing items by their fixed labels only. The client is also asked, on their own onboarding page, six plain-language questions (who hosts the site, where DNS is, where the domain was bought, how they sign in, whether they have a Google Business Profile, whether they use analytics); you see what they wrote as quoted text next to each checklist line, never as a status. The lead page links to its client record, and a lead with a site record is archived, never deleted.

One end-to-end test (`test/clients-flow.test.ts`) drives the real entry points (the signed Stripe webhook, the owner's routes under `/api`, the approvals route, the client's public `/o/<token>` and `/s/<token>` pages, the MCP tool `get_lead`, Today, the daily cron) against fakes for Stripe, the mail transport and the PDF browser, on a fixed calendar (Los Angeles, December 2026). It tells one story: a signed Growth proposal with Care and the client-hosted surcharge is paid by webhook, which makes the onboarding, one `primary` site (hosting tier `client_hosted`) and six `needed` items plus the six client questions; the client answers two on the public page (a password-looking answer to a third is refused with the neutral sentence, and the item stays empty); Today has no row at day 2 or a minute short of day 3, and has one at day 4 naming all six; Logan records `requested`, then `received` with a vault name and a note on four items and `not_applicable` on two (the row narrows to "Analytics", then clears); five secret-looking vault names and a secret-looking note are refused with a plain sentence that never repeats them, and the stored row is unchanged; after the build is finished the Care subscription is approved, opened by the client, paid at Checkout and activated by webhook, and `/api/clients` shows "Care $129/mo" and 12900 MRR; `client_hosted_no_access` shows until `accessMethod` is set to `mainwp`; the vault names and the owner notes appear on the owner's client record and are proven absent from the client's public onboarding page, every mail the fake transport saw (the welcome and two Friday updates among them, and the Care link), every approval the story made, the `get_lead` MCP output, Today, the client list, the lead detail and the whole timeline; deleting the lead is refused with 409; putting one item back to `needed` and the access method back to `none` shows both flags and a Today row, and archiving the site removes the flags, the Today row and the list's counts (a second archive is a 404, the history stays, the lead is still not deletable); a second client with only a hand-made site is also refused deletion ("this lead has a site record"), even once that site is archived. The flow found no defect in the code it exercises. Every page was only checked by typecheck, build, unit tests and a throwaway local rig, never against the real Worker, real session or real data.

## Decisions for Logan

1. A client is a won lead, or a lead with a project. There is no separate organization table: the business is the client and the people stay as they were.
2. Six fixed access items per site. Google Business Profile and analytics are repeated per site (mark one not applicable when it does not apply). Statuses are `needed`, `requested`, `received`, `verified`, `not_applicable`. The vault name is optional. `requested_at` keeps the FIRST ask, so do not read it as "waiting since".
3. The vault name and note heuristic. A name is the NAME of a password manager item. A name or note is refused when it has a JWT, a cloud, payment, GitHub, Slack, OpenAI-style or Google key prefix, a private key header, a long hex string, a "password: ..." style label, or a link with a login in it. A NAME is also refused when it is one word of 16 or more characters with three kinds of character (a random password), and a slug-style name such as "Acme-Plumbing-Cloudflare-DNS" is refused for that reason; the answer is a hint to use spaces. The single-word rule applies to names only: a bare password typed into a NOTE is not caught, and the form tells you never to type one. A name is also not a link.
4. Care status is derived from the client's `active` AND `past_due` subscriptions, so a client who is late still shows Care plus a "Payment late" badge. MRR counts the same two states and never a test row.
5. "Test client" means a business with only test rows (a test site, project or subscription and no real one). `businesses` has no test column, so this is computed; it is exported as `testClientSql` and Plan 10 must use the same rule. Test clients are left out of the list, MRR and Today (add `?test=1` to the list to see them).
6. `access_missing` is its own Today row, sitting under any billing row for the same client (priority 65 against 90, 75 and 66), and fires when a live, non-test project is 3 or more days old and a live site still has unresolved items. It names the fixed labels only, never a vault name, note or the client's words.
7. The six client-facing questions (Task 8) now go to EVERY onboarding with a job in it (a build, Care, a quick fix, SEO, reviews), so Care-only and quick-fix clients are no longer "light with no items". Their reminders, the Friday email and the 14-day silence rule now apply to them until they answer or you mark the items not applicable. Hourly work and the client-hosted surcharge alone get none. A client's answer is shown to you only as quoted text, "Client wrote (may be wrong)", never as a status. One shared neutral message ("This looks like it has a password, key or login in it...") is shown for every refused answer, on the client's page.
8. Task 6 added the Clients entry to the Projects rail and the site and access timeline titles. Editing or archiving a site logs `site_changed` with the field NAMES only (never a value); a status change logs `access_changed` with the label and the status word only.
9. The hosting tier is seeded from the signed packages (`client_hosted_surcharge` makes it `client_hosted`; a build or Care makes it `logan_hosted`; anything else leaves it `unknown`), and an owner edit is never overwritten by a later seed. An archived primary site is left archived (no new one is made). A hand-added site with the same domain is adopted as the primary. The seed also strips a login from a lead's website address before it stores it.
10. Not built, on purpose: a health score or grade, monitoring status, the "let me move you" draft for a client-hosted site, MCP tools for clients and sites (Plan 10: `get_client` and `get_site`, which must omit the vault names and notes), and a contact-role vocabulary.
11. Cut line (spec §12 six-week rule), not used: nothing was dropped. The client-facing questions (Task 8) were the line: if the branch has to shrink, drop them first (the owner's checklist works without them), which also restores the Plan 6 rule that a light onboarding has no items.

## Known gaps (decided, not fixed)

Client page and its data layer
- `workError()` in `src/client/workData.ts` can still show "invalid body" or "HTTP 500" on the timeline note error path.
- The `failed` banner on the client page is not reset on the same render as an id change.
- The page-level stale-load guard has no automated test (the `latestOnly` helper is tested; the page was only rig-checked).
- The Care label on the list is a second read (acceptable).
- Dates are formatted inconsistently on the client page, and the page fetches `/settings` twice.
- The table-to-cards CSS drops table semantics on mobile.

Site rules and seeding
- A schemeless `admin:pw@ace.com` in a lead's `website_url` is not stripped, and a login in a URL query string (`?token=`) is not caught.
- In `seed.ts`, the activity log is written after the site is created, so a thrown `site_added` log loses the line (the site stays).
- `vault_item` has no database length cap (the routes enforce 80).
- A bare password typed into a NOTE is not caught (see Decision 3); only the secret SHAPES are.
- Deleting a lead is refused by the earlier onboarding and project checks first, so the "has a site record" refusal is only reached by a lead with a site and nothing else (the flow test proves it with a hand-made site).

Tests
- `test/billing-flow.test.ts` has a 5 second timing flake under full-suite load (seen three times, unrelated to this plan); rerun it alone before blaming a change.
