# Plan 11 (Care monitoring: uptime, domain expiry, incidents, health): open items after the build

Branch `feat/agency-os-care-monitoring`, built 2026-11-21 by subagents: the implementers and the task reviewers on Sonnet, the final whole-branch review on Opus. One migration, `0061_monitoring` (three columns on `sites`, the `site_checks` and `site_incidents` tables). Nothing is deployed, pushed, merged or migrated remotely, and none of the pages was ever opened in a browser.

What was built, in four parts.

Probing and incidents. The existing 5-minute cron probes each monitored site once per tick (at most 40 a tick, the ones checked longest ago first, five at a time) with one GET that follows redirects and reads no body; the daily cron looks up each monitored domain's expiry from public RDAP (again after 7 days) and deletes checks older than 35 days. A site is monitored when it is real, live, has an address and its client has a live Care subscription, or you switched it on by hand; test clients, test sites, archived sites and sites with no address never are, and an address on a private or internal network is never fetched. A site is down on a network error, a timeout (10 seconds), a 5xx, a 404 or a 410 (a 401 or 403 is up; over 5 seconds is up and shown as slow); two checks in a row open one `down` incident, one good check closes it. A domain within 30 days of expiry (or past it) opens one `domain_expiring` incident and a later answer beyond 30 days closes it. Only a status code and a fixed phrase (`HTTP 503`, "could not connect", "timed out after 10 seconds") are ever stored; nothing a site or the registry sends back is. Opening or closing writes a timeline line (`site_down`, `site_back`, `domain_expiring`) and nothing else: no mail, no approval, no task, no Claude task.

Health and Today. A client's health is 0 to 100 and says why, from open down incidents (30), the nearest domain expiry (10 to 20), uptime under 99.5% (10) or 99.0% (20) once there are 20 checks, an overdue invoice (15), a billing hold (25), a failed subscription payment (15), missing site access (10) and waiting on the client (10); good is 80 or more, watch 60 to 79, at risk below 60. A client with an open incident gets one `site_incident` row on Today: a site that is down at priority 90 ("ace.com has been down for 47 minutes"), a domain close to expiring at 66 ("ace.com expires in 12 days"); the number of days is always worked out from the site's expiry date, never from the text stored when the incident opened.

Routes, MCP and pages. `GET /api/care` (the board: real clients on Care or with a site switched on, worst score first), `GET /api/incidents` (open or closed, one client or all), `GET /api/sites/:id/checks` and `PUT /api/sites/:id/monitoring`. The `agency-os` MCP server now has 27 tools: the read-only `get_care_overview` and `list_incidents`, which show a domain and never an address, and no vault name, access note, token or stored incident text. The pages: `/care` (the board), a Care panel on the client page (health chip with its band in words, each site's state, uptime and domain expiry, the monitoring switch, the last five incidents) and a health column.

End-to-end test. `test/care-monitoring-flow.test.ts` drives the real entry points (the 5-minute cron, `sweepDomains` called directly, the owner's routes under `/api`, `GET /api/today`, the MCP route) on a fixed calendar (Los Angeles, Thursday 3 December 2026, 09:00). The only fakes are `monitoringDeps.fetch` (a recorder that answers each site and each RDAP lookup) and the mail transport (which must see nothing). It tells one story: a client with Care, a second Care client with a healthy site, an archived site and a site on a private address, a client without Care, and a test client with Care. Tick 1 (200) writes a check and opens nothing; tick 2 (503) writes a check, opens nothing and Today has no row; tick 3 (503) opens one incident, Today has one `site_incident` at priority 90 with the exact reason, the board shows the client first, down, 30 points lower, `get_care_overview` and `list_incidents` agree with it and the timeline has one `site_down`; tick 4 (503) changes nothing; tick 5 (200) closes it, Today is empty, `site_back` is logged once ("back after 10 min") and the score is back where it started. The recorder shows only the two Care sites were ever fetched (not the client without Care, the test client's site, the archived site or the private address, which got no check). `sweepDomains` with a fake RDAP answer 12 days out opens `domain_expiring` and Today shows it at priority 66 ("expires in 12 days"); an answer of 400 days closes it and `GET /api/incidents` shows the closed one as "domain expiry warning". Switching the no-Care client on through `PUT /api/sites/:id/monitoring` makes the next tick fetch it (and off again, not); a private address and a test client's site are refused. At the end the number of approvals, tasks and Claude tasks is what it was before the first tick and the mail transport saw nothing; no address, token, vault name or site response is in any MCP answer or board route; every number on the board is finite. The daily cron is deliberately not used for the domain steps (it also runs the onboarding, project and billing sweeps, which can make approvals and tasks of their own), so `runScheduled(env, DAILY_CRON, ...)` is covered only by the plan's earlier schedule tests. The flow test found no defect in the code it exercises. Every page (`/care`, the client Care panel, the health column) was only checked by typecheck, build, unit tests and component helpers, never against the real Worker, a real session or real data.

## Decisions for Logan

The eleven calls the plan made:

1. Plan 11 is monitoring, incidents and health only; the rest of Phase 3 is Plans 12 to 15.
2. A probe is one GET of the site's URL from the Worker, following redirects. Down means a network error, a timeout (10 s), a 5xx, a 404 or a 410. A 401 or 403 counts as up (the server answered; a firewall that blocks the Worker would otherwise page you all night). A response slower than 5 s is up and is only shown as slow. NEEDS YOUR CALL.
3. Two checks in a row (5 to 10 minutes) open a down incident; one good check closes it. NEEDS YOUR CALL.
4. The Worker cannot read a TLS certificate's expiry, so certificate expiry is not monitored: an expired certificate shows as down (the connection fails). Reading expiry properly is a Mac helper job and goes in Plan 12.
5. Domain expiry is read weekly from public RDAP (`https://rdap.org/domain/<domain>`). Within 30 days of expiry opens a `domain_expiring` incident; a domain RDAP does not know or cannot answer for gives no incident, only "unknown".
6. A site is monitored when it is real, live, has a URL, and its client has a live Care subscription (the same rule `careLabel` uses) or you switched monitoring on for that site. A client without Care is never probed unless you switch it on.
7. There is no push or email alert for a down site: Today, the client page, the Care board and MCP only. Mailing yourself unattended would be the app's first unattended send, so it is not built. Say so if a morning look is too slow for a down site. NEEDS YOUR CALL.
8. Health is 0 to 100 from the deductions above (down 30, domain 10 to 20, uptime 10 to 20, overdue invoice 15, billing hold 25, past-due subscription 15, access missing 10, waiting on client 10), bands good 80 or more, watch 60 to 79, at risk below 60. These numbers are a first guess; the formula is one small function. NEEDS YOUR CALL.
9. An incident is a record, not a task: no task is created. The "incident task" of CARE-5 comes with the update batch in Plan 12.
10. Client grade (A/B/C) and capacity are deferred to Plan 15: their cutoffs are your calls and nothing here needs them.
11. Cut line (spec section 12, six-week rule), not used: nothing was dropped. If the branch had to shrink, the pages (Task 9) were to go first, keeping the routes and the MCP tools, then the MCP tools (Task 8).

The rulings made while building (each is plain: what, and what it costs):

- R1. The plan's example of a domain 12 days out was really 13 (23:30 on 20 November in Los Angeles); the test and the plan were corrected. Domain days are counted in Los Angeles dates (the booking zone), never UTC.
- R2. `sweepDomains` reads at most 500 sites a day (`monitoredSites(db, 500)`). Costs: a fleet of more than 500 monitored sites skips the rest until the order changes.
- R3. `careFleet` and its view types (`CareRow`, `SiteMonitoringView`, `IncidentView`) live in `src/worker/monitoring/fleet.ts`, and `incidentView` there is the one function that words an incident, for the routes, Today, MCP and the pages.
- R4. A domain incident's stored sentence is frozen when it opens, so every reader works the days out again from the site's expiry date; an open one reads "domain expires in 12 days", a closed one the fixed phrase "domain expiry warning" (a renewed domain would otherwise show a number that is no longer true).
- R5. The flow test calls `sweepDomains` directly and counts approvals and tasks around the monitoring calls only, because the daily cron also runs the onboarding, project and billing sweeps.
- R6. A subscription counts as "failed payment" for health when it is any real past-due subscription, not only Care: a failed Local SEO or Reviews payment also lowers health ("A subscription payment failed"). Waiting on the client is true if any open real onboarding is waiting (a health signal, not Today's one-reason display).
- R7. A client with an open site incident is a Today candidate even with no live project (a fix to the candidate query), and `site_down`, `site_back` and `domain_expiring` are timeline lines that do not count as contacting a lead. The priorities 90 and 66 tie with a billing hold (90) and a drafted invoice (66); the stable sort keeps billing first. A client with several sites down gets one row ("and 1 other site is down too").
- R8. Incident lists (Today, `GET /api/incidents`, MCP `list_incidents`, the board) leave out an incident on an archived site or a site with no address, in the shared query. Costs: an open incident on a site whose address was cleared or that was archived is hidden everywhere instead of lingering until closed (see the known gap about its health deduction).
- R9. A site whose address the probe may not fetch (private network, internal name) is skipped, writes no check and opens nothing; switching it on answers 409. It is not a full defence against a request to an internal address (see the known gaps).
- R10. The health chip says its band in words ("Health 82 · good"), not by colour alone.

## Known gaps (decided, not fixed)

Probe and sweeps
- Starvation: a monitored site with a non-monitorable address writes no check, so it is always first in the order; with 40 or more of them the real sites are never probed. The decided fix (not built): read five times the tick limit, validate, then slice to 40, without recording failed checks (that would open incidents).
- `sweepDomains` stamps `domain_checked_at` even when the registry answer failed or was rate limited, so a failed lookup waits 7 days; its 500-row read is ordered by uptime-check age, not domain age.
- A site whose `recordProbe` throws is counted neither as probed nor as failed. `openIncident` runs on every failing tick after the second (a no-op, but a query each time). The incident write and its timeline line are two statements, so a throw between them loses the line.
- `openIncident` re-reads the row after the insert (a small race): use RETURNING with a null check, and add a concurrent-open test.
- `probe.ts` blocks all of 192.0.0.0/16 where its comment says only two /24s; a probed address keeps any user name and password in the URL (refuse or strip them); redirect-to-internal and DNS-to-private are not defended (the Worker cannot reach private networks, but this is not a full defence against a request to an internal address); the abort-and-timer pattern is written twice (probe and RDAP); a dead branch in `parseIPv6`.
- The inline `UPDATE sites SET domain_checked_at` in the incident engine should be a helper in `db/monitoring.ts`; `logActivity` has no time parameter (the timeline line uses the real clock).

Health
- The uptime reason prints the raw figure (99.0 shows as "99%"): use one decimal ("Uptime 98.7%"). The live-sites query has no non-empty-address filter, so a client's health still deducts 30 for an open down incident on a site whose address was cleared, an incident every list now hides and nothing closes. The decided fix (one clause and a test, and consider closing a site's open incidents when its address is cleared or it is archived) was left for the final wave.
- Two definitions of "a site the owner sees" (`BOARD_SITE` in `fleet.ts` and the `listIncidents` WHERE) and "has Care" derived three ways (`careLabel`, `CARE_SUBSCRIPTION_SQL` three times) can drift: a shared SQL constant and a drift test would stop it. `healthInputs` counts the checks and incidents of a site with no address that the board hides.
- A domain incident with no known expiry date reads "domain is close to expiring" (clumsy, untested); equal days and equal open times fall back to the list order (add the site id as a tiebreak); the 1000-row cap on the incident read is undocumented; `outageLength` is exported but used only in `today.ts`.
- `healthInputs` and the record, health-data and fleet modules import each other in a cycle (safe today, only function-body uses); a leaf module for `testClientSql`, `LIVE`, `careLabel`, `clientMrrCents` and `isOverdue` would end it. The new Today candidate query does not exclude test clients (an import cycle with `testClientSql`); harmless, because the incident attention leaves them out.

Routes and MCP
- `GET /api/sites/:id/checks` passes the stored error text through unsanitised (the stored values are only fixed phrases); apply the same whitelist as the incident view. `GET /api/incidents` has no paging beyond its limit.
- MCP: the client names in `list_incidents` use inline SQL (a db helper would be tidier); no test for an archived-site exclusion or a hostile `clientId` such as `' OR 1=1 --` (the value is bound, never built into the query); `search_clients` now pays a health read per call.
- Missing tests: an ended (cancelled) Care subscription staying off the board in a route test, the other-4xx-is-up and 499/500 boundary cases, "timeout is not early", the RDAP timer leak, a morning case and a daylight-saving case for the domain days, a non-Care past-due package, a client whose real site is last in the over-50-ids chunk, two onboardings, an all-archived-sites client; the constants test is a literal check and one length assertion is trivially true; the health tests share a file with the `GET /api/health` smoke test; the incident guard test asserts four cases together.

Pages
- Never opened in a browser (see Needs Logan). The state chip derives its colour from the label text (a `siteStateTone` function in `careView.ts` with a test would be sounder); the switch label "Monitoring is off" contradicts a Care client's checked site (reword "Switched on by hand"); the switch's `disabled` while a request is in flight drops keyboard focus (use `aria-disabled`); the page builds `new Date()` per render, so "down for N minutes" is stale until the next render; the panel fetches all incidents and slices five in the browser; the shared chips live in `CarePanel.tsx` and `Care.tsx` imports them (a `careParts.tsx` would be cleaner); a uptime of 99.96 or 0.04 is unreachable but would round to "100%" or "0%"; the Refresh button has no in-flight state; the 59 and 65 minute boundaries are not unit-tested.
- `careView.ts` value-imports `FALLBACK_ZONE` from the SOP cadence module, which pulls the booking config and slots into the client bundle (`billingData.ts` does the same; it predates this plan).
- Shell.tsx's kind-to-label ternary grows by one case per Today kind.

Tests
- The full suite has flakes that are timing or data, not this plan (each passes alone): `billing-flow` and `billing-sweep` time out (5 s) under full-suite load, `projects-flow` has a timing flake, the MCP rate-limit test can trip when it runs alongside other MCP tests, and `sites-onboarding-items` ('password: abc') fails when a random UUID contains "abc".
- The flow test pins its clock with fake `Date` only, so a probe's real timers (the 10-second cap) are real; every fake answer is immediate, so none fires.
