What was built, in nine parts.

Grade. Each client has a grade A, B or C that is yours; the app only suggests one by a fixed rule and always shows why (`src/worker/clients/grade.ts`, `gradeData.ts`). C is checked first: health under 60, or two late invoices in 12 months, or three signed change orders in six. A needs a monthly revenue of 129 dollars or more, a health of 80 or better (and known), no late invoice in 12 months, at most one change order in six months and a tenure of 60 days. Everything else is B. The chip shows the letter and, when you have not set one, the word "suggested" in a lighter style. The Clients table gained a ninth column (Grade). `PUT /api/clients/:id/grade` sets or clears it with a note.

Capacity. `GET /api/capacity` and the second half of `GET /api/today` read how much of your time is booked this week and the next three (`src/worker/capacity/compute.ts`): the estimates of your open tasks due in that week, yours in full, "Claude drafts, you approve" tasks at a quarter, Claude's own not at all; work overdue from earlier weeks is added to this week and shown as carry-over; waiting-on-client and undated tasks count nothing. "Near" is 80 percent of the limit. The limit is 20 hours a week and 45 clients, set in Settings > Work. It only warns: a Home card, a line on Money ("31 of 45 clients"), and a sentence at the bottom of a proposal-send approval. Nothing is blocked.

The batch board. `GET /api/batches`, the page `/batches` and the MCP tool `list_batches`: for each recurring template (Care weekly and monthly, Local SEO monthly, quarterly and annual), the tasks of the current period across live real clients, with the grade first (A, B, C, none), a billing-hold flag, a progress line ("3 of 11 done, about 4 h 20 m left"), and a "Work through" button to the next open task. The period before this one shows only while it still has an open task. Read only: no bulk done or skip.

The post-launch ladder. When a build project is finished (through any path that runs the project-done hooks), five dated rows are made for the client: review request on the launch day, video testimonial after 10 days, thank-you package after 30, three-month check-in after 90, case-study prompt after 120; plus a December letter on 1 December each year for live clients (`src/worker/ladder/`). Dates are calendar days in the booking zone, added as days (never months). The daily cron (the last part of it) fills in what the hook missed (builds finished in the last 180 days), skips what is too late (45 days, the December letter after 31 December) with a plain note, marks the thank-you and case-study steps `due` (your task, no mail), and drafts the review request, testimonial, check-in and December mail as `ladder.mail` approvals (at most 10 a run). Approving one sends a fixed-template mail from the main mailbox to the client's contact, once, and marks the step done. The Google review link is `https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=<place id>` from the client's Local SEO profile, or a link you paste on the row. A held, rehearsal, ended, or do-not-contact client is never mailed. The client's ladder panel, a Today row (priority 56) for a step waiting on you, `GET /api/clients/:id/ladder`, `POST /api/ladder/:id/done` and `/skip`, `PUT /api/ladder/:id/link`.

Auto-approve. Two fixed kinds, both OFF until you switch them on at `/settings/auto-approve` (`src/worker/approvals/auto.ts`): `invoice.reminder` for every step except the last, and `onboarding.nudge` for reminder levels 1 to 3. Never: the last invoice reminder (it names the pause date), onboarding level 4, the Friday update, the pause notice, anything Claude wrote, a test client, a client on hold. Only approvals created after the switch was turned on (the switch time is the cut-off). At most 20 a local day. Each goes through the ordinary `approve()` (there is no second send path), is listed on the page with its outcome and leaves one `auto_approved` line on the client's timeline. A failed send leaves the reminder pending with its error.

Studio. `/studio` and `/api/studio`: three post kinds. An audit tip (a carousel of up to six slides from a real audit's findings, anonymous by default: the business, its site, phone and address are scrubbed before Claude sees the facts, and a result that names the client is refused), a before and after (you upload two images of 1 MB or less each, PNG, JPEG or WebP, checked by magic bytes) and a case study card (from finished work). Claude writes a headline, a caption, up to six slides and up to five tags through the queue (`studio.copy`, Sonnet; fixed instructions, facts only in the untrusted block; the same word guards as the Local SEO report: no link, price, markdown or promise). The copy is saved, the images are drawn (1080 by 1350 PNG, best effort), and one `studio.post` approval is proposed. Approving only marks the post approved; you download the slides, post by hand and press "mark posted". Pictures leave the bucket only through the authenticated image route; no JSON ever holds an R2 key. The MCP tool `get_studio_posts` shows words and counts, never a key and never the client's name on an anonymous post.

The kickoff deck. `GET /api/projects/:id/kickoff-deck.pdf` draws a PDF for a client's kickoff from the project's own steps: for each phase, what we do and what we need from you (no dates, no step titles, only the client-facing summaries). It uses the recommendation deck's renderer and 501s without the Browser Rendering binding.

Owner pages. `/studio`, `/batches`, `/settings/auto-approve`, the ladder panel on the client record, the grade picker and chip, the Capacity card on Home, the capacity fields in Settings > Work, and links from the sidebar.

End-to-end test. `test/growth-flow.test.ts` drives the real entry points on a fixed calendar (Los Angeles): a real client with a Local SEO place id and a Care project; its build's last step is finished through `POST /api/tasks/:id/complete` (five ladder rows, dated from the launch day); the daily cron on launch day drafts the review request as a `ladder.mail` approval and mails nothing; the owner approves it through `POST /api/approvals/:id/approve` and exactly one message goes to the contact, with the review link made from the place id; ten days later the cron drafts the testimonial request; thirty days after launch `GET /api/today` carries a `ladder_due` row for the thank-you package and `POST /api/ladder/:id/done` clears it; `GET /api/batches` lists the client under the Care template for the week once the real project sweep made the task (and not the old weeks), and `GET /api/capacity` counts it (and carries the old weeks over); with `PUT /api/auto-approve/invoice.reminder` on, a real open invoice goes through the daily cron's own billing sweep: reminder 1 is sent through the transport fake with nobody pressing anything, and the last reminder waits pending until the owner approves it; a Studio audit tip goes from an anonymous create from a real audit, to Claude's claim through the MCP route (the business's name, site and phone are in neither the instructions nor the untrusted block), a refused result ("guarantee"), a valid result through `complete_claude_task`, the one `studio.post` approval, the owner's approval and `mark posted`; no response, MCP answer or approval card holds an R2 key or the business's name; nothing was logged as an error. The only fakes are the registry lookup the daily cron makes, the mail transport and the image drawing (the real `renderPostImages`, with a stand-in PNG function instead of the browser). The flow test passed on its first full run: **it found no defect** in Plan 15's code. (What it had to learn about the app, in case you rehearse by hand: the owner's session does not last a month, so after a long jump in time the test logs in again; and the daily cron on 3 December makes that week's Care task, on 13 December the next week's, so the capacity card carries earlier weeks' open Care tasks over as overdue.)

## Rulings made while building

Each is plain: what, and what it costs. Numbered in the order the ledger recorded them.

Before the build (the ledger's pre-flight).
- R1. Task 7 adds no MCP tool of its own. The plan's scope said three new MCP tools, but the tasks make two (`list_batches`, `get_studio_posts`), so the plan was edited and the count is 32. Costs nothing.
- R2. An "ended" client, for the ladder, is a client with at least one subscription and all of them cancelled or ended. A build-only client (no subscription at all) is not ended: it still gets its dated steps. The December letter needs an active or past-due subscription. The plan was edited to say so. Costs: if this is wrong, a build-only client misses its review request.
- R3. The case-study button on the ladder panel (Task 6) is written against Task 9's `POST /api/studio`; between those two tasks it was a dead button. No test depended on it. Costs nothing now.

During the build.
- R4. (Task 4) "Work through" skips a held client's tasks, where the brief said the first open task in the order shown: a hold stops the work by the contract. Held tasks still count in the group's open count and its minutes (capacity leaves them out), so a group's "about X left" is a little high. A one-line revert in `firstOpenTask` if you want the brief's way.
- R5. (Task 11) The kickoff deck's "What we need from you" is the steps with a client summary that wait on the client; the data has no field for a step the client owns (owner tags are only `claude`, `logan` and `claude_drafts`). A phase with steps but no client summaries gets one fixed line ("We will walk you through this part when we get to it."). Costs: the deck is thin, and that line is borderline a promise.
- R6. (Tasks 8 and 9) Studio errors: an upload or render refusal is a 422 with a plain sentence; a missing `BROWSER` binding is a 501 (like the recommendation deck's PDF route), through a small subclass in `studio/images.ts`. The route also checks the upload slot (`before` or `after`) at run time, so a slot like `1.png` can never overwrite a drawn slide.
- R7. (Task 2) A needs known health (above). The late invoice is a real invoice made in the last 12 months that has a reminder step past its due offset; change orders are signed ones made in the last six months; tenure is the earlier of the first real project start and the first real paid invoice. `listClients` now reads the health inputs for every client (it did only for Care clients), at the same number of queries per chunk.
- R8. (Task 6) A drafted ladder step can be marked done or skipped, and doing so turns down its pending approval in the same step (the brief said only due or waiting; without it a rejected mail was a dead row). A link can be changed only while a step is waiting or due. A mail held at approval time (a hold, a test client, an ended client) sends nothing, and the step goes to `due` with a note so it shows on Today.
- R9. (Task 6, fix round 1) A drafted step never stays behind a decided approval. A rejected approval (turned down in the inbox) moves its step to `due` with "the mail was turned down in Approvals" at the next sweep; an already-approved one is never adopted again. A review request that already had a mail drafted does not go back to `waiting` when a link is saved. Costs: a step held at approval time is closed by hand (send it yourself, then mark it done, or skip it); the panel says so.
- R10. (Task 7) The 20-a-day cap is atomic: a place is reserved with one statement before the send, so two overlapping sweeps cannot pass 20; a failed attempt counts toward the 20. `setRule` keeps its old switch-on time when a rule that is already on is switched on again (the time is the cut-off), and restamps it after an off and on.
- R11. (Task 8, fix round 1) Long Studio copy used to clip the headline off the top of a slide. Costs: the layout was never seen in a real browser.

A result from Claude that names the client of an anonymous post is refused at completion (the task stays claimed). `studio.post` is listed among the approvals Claude may propose over MCP. The shared word guards moved to `queue/wordGuards.ts` and now also refuse "best in". Costs: a business whose first word is a common word will hit the refusal more often (turn anonymity off with the client's agreement, or write the post yourself).

## Rollback
The Worker can be rolled back safely onto the new schema once the migrations are applied: the code from before this plan ignores the four new tables and the grade columns and has no `/studio`, `/batches` or ladder pages. The schema itself is rolled back only through the D1 Time Travel bookmark recorded before migrating (Needs Logan, item 1). A mail already sent (a review request, an auto-approved reminder) stays in the client's inbox. The `ladder.mail` approvals the first cron drafts stay pending after a rollback; with the old Worker live they have no handler and cannot be approved, and they work again when the new Worker is back. Switching every auto-approve rule off on `/settings/auto-approve` is the quick way to stop sends without a rollback.
