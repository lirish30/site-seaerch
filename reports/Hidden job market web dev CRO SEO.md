# Reach hiring owners before anyone posts

Build a thin careers-page watcher on top of the scanner you already have, but do not build a standalone bulk careers search tool first. The tool is feasible, and the ATS (applicant tracking system) feeds are public. A careers watcher would still find roles that are public on the employer's own site, and free tools like Hiring.cafe already index them. The scarce thing is a fast, specific message to the person who owns the problem. In the best-documented dataset, referred candidates reach interview 40% of the time against 3% for inbound applicants ([Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals)). Lever's data shows proactively sourced candidates were hired about twice as often as career-site applicants, 1 in 72 against 1 in 152 ([SHRM on Lever](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/lever-study-shows-1-100-candidates-hired)). The popular "70-80% of jobs are hidden" claim is untraceable, so do not repeat it. The best plan stacks four things your tool is already close to doing: detect a need (a stale posting, active ad spend, a failing site, a new marketing leader), run the audit, email the owner within hours, and pitch contract work and a job in the same email. There is no independent reply-rate data for this niche. Every number you will see for audit-led outreach comes from vendors, forum threads or one-person case studies, so plan to run your own 200-300 contact test to get real numbers.

## What to do this week, what to build, what to skip

The ordering below is my judgment, built on the evidence in the sections that follow. Steps 1-3 cost nothing or close to it. Step 4 is the only real build.

| # | When | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 1 | Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC (`p=none` is enough) on a dedicated outreach subdomain, and put a postal address and a one-line opt-out in the footer of anything that pitches services. | Google says all senders need authentication, and enforcement ramped up from November 2025 ([Google FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)). CAN-SPAM has no B2B exemption, and a freelance pitch is probably commercial ([FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). |

| 3 | Nov 2 | Run quoted-phrase Algolia queries on the new HN "Who is hiring?" thread, then email the poster directly. | The live thread had 387 comments in October, and an exact-phrase "SEO" query returned only 4 hits, so it is a small and cheap channel ([HN Algolia](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22SEO%22&tags=comment,story_49922569)). |
| 4 | This week to next | Build the ATS watcher (see the third section). Start with Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, run it against the company domains already in your leads table, and log `first_seen`. | It reuses your data, and it is the only way to measure your actual lead over LinkedIn. |
| 5 | Weeks 2-3 | Add one demand signal to the scanner score: Google Ads Transparency Center lookup by domain, then audit the ad landing page rather than the homepage. | Active ad spend indicates budget, and a broken landing page is a CRO problem you can show (see the fourth section). |
| 6 | Weeks 2-4 | Send 200-300 hand-reviewed, audit-led emails split across two or three segments, and track reply, call and paid outcomes yourself. | No independent benchmark exists, so your own numbers are the only trustworthy ones. |

Skip these for now:

- **Workday, JazzHR, Rippling and iCIMS.** Workday is only useful for large enterprises, and the others have no confirmed public feed or were not researched.
- **Scraping LinkedIn, Indeed or Glassdoor.** The legal risk is the highest of any option here. LinkedIn won an enforceable-contract ruling against hiQ and a $500,000 consent judgment ([Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/)).
- **Storing Google Places results as a lead database.** Secondary sources say Google's terms bar it ([OpenPlacesAPI](https://openplacesapi.com/blog/can-you-store-places-api-results)). Get legal review first, or use licensed data or state bulk files instead.
- **BuiltWith at about $295 per month, sequencing platforms like Instantly, the X API and Reddit's commercial API.** These are not worth it at job-search volume. A volume sender is the use case they are built for, and large lists perform worse (2.1% reply at 1,000+ recipients against 5.8% for under 50, per a secondary summary of Belkins data, [Indie Hackers](https://indiehackers.com/post/329774e623)).
- **Unsolicited full redesign mockups.** The one published test returned 0% ([UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/i-tried-this-viral-strategy-to-find-design-clients-part-01-ed810699c5eb)). Give away the diagnosis and quick wins, and keep implementation behind the conversation.

## The hidden market is real but small, and referral advantage is the evidence that holds

Nobody can trace the 70-80% figure. A university career center says it is "difficult to trace to a single source" and probably comes from 1970s-80s career-counseling lore ([UCF Career Services](https://career.ucf.edu/blog/2024/08/12/is-it-true-that-80-of-job-openings-are-not-advertised/)). The better-documented numbers are smaller. Referrals were roughly 11-12% of hires in CareerPlug's small-business data and Breezy's 2024 report (both seen only via search summaries, [Breezy](https://breezy.hr/resources/hiring-reports/source-of-hire-report-2024)), and one financial-services firm showed 29% (a single-firm study reported by [Quartz](https://qz.com/299923/why-job-referrals-matter)). Applying to a posted job still produced **43-52% of hires** in Ashby's data across 250K hires ([Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report)). Ashby's customer base is tech-heavy, so these figures may not generalize to agencies or small businesses. Roles without a public posting exist, but no measurement says how many.

What the data does support is an arbitrage on a scarce channel. Referrals were only about 1% of applications in Ashby's 38M-application dataset, yet they reached interview at 40% against 3% for inbound, and 16% of interviewed referrals got offers against 6% ([Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals)). Part of that gap is selection, because referrers screen, so an unreferred cold email will land somewhere between the two rates, not at 40%. Two other facts widen the opening. Ashby reports applications per hire have tripled since 2021 ([press release via search summary](https://www.mydailyrecord.com/online_features/press_releases/new-data-from-ashby-reveals-surge-in-applications-rising-selectivity-and-shifting-recruiter-workloads/article_7148b495-d075-50c8-9b94-90cd4fe18dcd.html)), which backs your "saturated" read. Greenhouse classes 18-22% of postings on its platform as ghost jobs (the method is not explained, [Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/greenhouse-2024-state-of-job-hunting-report)). A direct reply from an owner proves a live need, so it sidesteps both problems.

Freelance demand is the same story. Upwork's active clients fell 4% year over year to 763,000 in Q2 2026, and management cut its outlook citing AI automation ([PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/earnings/2026/upwork-navigates-cross-currents-as-ai-reshapes-freelance-demand)). Freelancers in the surveys the notes found rank referrals and word of mouth far above online bidding (27% and 24% against 6%, weak provenance, [Make a Living Writing](https://makealivingwriting.com/what-freelancers-earn-and-how-they-find-client/)). Boards are crowded for both kinds of work, which supports going direct.

## A careers watcher is cheap to build, and the seed list is the moat

The ATS feeds are public by design. Greenhouse states that "Job Board data is publicly available, so authentication is not required for any GET endpoints" ([Greenhouse docs](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html)). The hard part is knowing which company uses which board, because no ATS publishes a customer directory ([Apify](https://apify.com/groundtruth/ats-job-listings)). Hiring.cafe's creator reportedly spent weeks hand-curating about 100,000 career pages and then scrapes them about three times a day with an LLM extracting fields ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qy7a89/snapshots/2026-02-08T16%3A30%3A19.41958Z), self-reported). Your seed list is the scanner's existing leads table. Fetch each domain's careers page, regex for ATS hostnames, confirm by calling the API, and store `{company, ats, token, last_ok}`. The notes call this the seed method; it is design inference, not a documented recipe.

| ATS | Endpoint pattern | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Greenhouse | `GET https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{token}/jobs?content=true` | Official docs |
| Lever | `GET https://api.lever.co/v0/postings/{site}?mode=json` (EU: `api.eu.lever.co`) | Official README |
| Ashby | `GET https://api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/{name}?includeCompensation=true` (filter `isListed: false`) | Official docs |
| Workable | `https://apply.workable.com/api/v1/widget/accounts/{subdomain}` | Vendor-reported; another source gives a different path, so test it |
| Recruitee | `https://{company}.recruitee.com/api/offers/` | Vendor-reported |
| Breezy | `https://{company}.breezy.hr/json` | Vendor-reported; not every customer enables it |
| SmartRecruiters | `https://api.smartrecruiters.com/v1/companies/{id}/postings` | Vendor-reported |
| Teamtailor | append `.rss` to the jobs page | Official support guide |
| BambooHR | `{company}.bamboohr.com/careers/list` | Vendor-reported |
| Workday | `POST https://{tenant}.wd{N}.myworkdayjobs.com/wday/cxs/{tenant}/{site}/jobs` | Undocumented; `limit` must be 20 |

Verify any endpoint with one live `curl` before building on it. Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby are stable and cover startups and tech companies. The small-agency tier you care about mostly sits on Workable, Recruitee, BambooHR and Breezy, so add those in the second pass. Two build details matter. First, the monitor will see an empty or failed board response as mass job closure, so require two consecutive empty responses before marking a role closed ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/greenhouse-api-for-many-companies)). Second, key dedupe on `(ats, board, job_id)`, and keep your own `first_seen`, since Workday's `postedOn` is a localized string.

How much lead this buys you over LinkedIn is unmeasured. Vendors claim career pages are live at hour 0 and LinkedIn lags 18-72 hours ([Jobstrack](https://jobstrack.io/blog/linkedin-job-posting-delay)), while another claims 6-24 hours ([Scoutify](https://scoutify.com/blog/how-to-find-jobs-before-linkedin/)). Both are marketing and neither shows method. Companies that post to LinkedIn directly have no lag at all. Logging `first_seen` against the posting's own timestamp and a sample of LinkedIn "posted" times will give you your own answer within a couple of weeks. The notes estimate **5-8 focused days** for a first version covering the main ATSs and a few dollars a month on Cloudflare Workers plus D1, but Cloudflare pricing was not checked and the build estimate is the researcher's guess. A version bolted onto your existing leads table should be smaller. Treat that as my judgment. Skip Common Crawl harvesting, Google dorks and JobPosting JSON-LD crawling at first. Common Crawl is sampled and months stale, and the dork recall is poor.

The best use of the watcher is not "find postings first". It is to turn each posting into a signal. A web, SEO or CRO role open for 30-60 days, or reposted, means the company has the pain, and ghost-job rates mean it may not be filling it. The pitch writes itself: "I can cover this now while you hire, and here is what I found on your site." Your scanner and drafter produce the audit. This single idea connects the job search and the freelance search, and it is an inference with no evidence on conversion behind it. Also watch for the first Ashby or Greenhouse board a small company ever creates, and for adjacent hires (a PPC or content hire with no SEO or web person). Both are unvalidated ideas from the notes.

## Demand signals beat generic lists, but the hit-rate evidence is almost all vendor claims

Rank signals by how directly they show budget or intent. My ranking, which is judgment and not measured, is: explicit "looking for" posts on Reddit or in communities first, then active ad spend pointing at a weak landing page, then a failing Core Web Vitals score on an established business, then a new marketing leader, then funding or a new company filing, then stale or adjacent job postings, then launches and events. The evidence is thin throughout. Vendor benchmarks claim signal-triggered outreach gets 8-15% total replies against 1-3% for generic, and double when four or more signals stack ([Rivereditor](https://rivereditor.com/blogs/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks-smb), [Unify](https://webflow.unifygtm.com/explore/signal-based-outreach-metrics)). None of that is a controlled experiment, and no benchmark exists for website-change signals specifically.

Funding is the weakest signal for a freelancer. Signalbase found a detectable post-funding hire in only 107 of 474 companies (23%), with a median of 35 days to the first hire, but its methodology section was empty ([Signalbase](https://www.trysignalbase.com/article/is-the-fundraising-signal-still-useful-in-2026)). Employbl's placebo test suggests much of the post-raise hiring spike is market-wide (+51% in non-raise quarters against +58% in raise quarters, [Employbl](https://www.employbl.com/blog/post-raise-hiring-spike-control-group), secondary report). Form D data is free from the SEC ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-d-data-sets)), but it skews to funds and shells, so use it as a list source, not a trigger. A new company with no site is a stronger need signal for contract work than any hiring inference.

Ads and site problems fit your tool best. Google's Ads Transparency Center is searchable by domain and shows ads from the last 30 days ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/meta-google-ads-libraries/)). Meta's API does not expose US-only commercial ads, so Meta is manual-UI only. CrUX field data is free and can be joined with HTTP Archive technology tables in BigQuery to list origins failing Core Web Vitals by platform ([HTTP Archive forum](https://discuss.httparchive.org/t/list-of-domains-using-a-given-technology/2755)). The caveat is that a slow site shows a need for fixes, not budget or intent.

For the new-marketing-leader signal, the only survey behind "new CMOs change agencies in 90 days" is a 2014 UK poll of 75 marketing directors, 70% of whom said they planned to ([Manifest](https://manifest.agency/2014/03/21/the-first-100-days-tenure-of-a-cmo/)). Do not quote it as current. A weekly diff of target companies' /team and /leadership pages is free and gives you a natural audit-as-welcome-gift opener.

Government accessibility work is a concrete deadline-driven niche for contract work. DOJ's April 2026 interim final rule pushed the ADA Title II web deadline to **April 26, 2027** for entities of 50,000 or more people and April 26, 2028 for smaller entities, with a WCAG 2.1 AA standard ([Plante Moran](https://plantemoran.com/explore-our-thinking/insight/2026/04/doj-extends-ada-title-ii-web-accessibility-compliance-deadlines)). Confirm the final rule status in the Federal Register. Municipal website RFPs are usually bundled (design, CMS, hosting, accessibility, support), which favors agencies, so a solo freelancer realistically wins audits, sub-pieces or subcontracts. Micro-purchase thresholds rose to $15,000 for federal purchases on October 1, 2025, and local limits vary ([NCMBC](https://www.ncmbc.us/far-thresholds-are-changing-on-october-1-2025/)). Frame outreach as a compliance-readiness audit. One source warns that an implied legal threat carries reputational risk.

## What reply rates to plan for, and what the evidence cannot tell you

Plan on single digits. The cold-email benchmarks that tracked actual sends are generic B2B: Instantly's 2025 platform average was 3.43% reply, with the top quartile at 5.5% and above ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)). For local-business web design the picture is worse. A forum thread had one agency owner at 9% positive replies, others at 1-3%, and one commenter said the list matters more than the copy ([Yo Motherboard](https://yomotherboard.com/question/how-effective-is-cold-emailing-for-web-agencies/)). A late-2025 Reddit post described roughly 10,000 leads with no positive responses ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1pba099/snapshots/2025-12-05T07%3A20%3A49.848045Z)). A vendor-reported web-studio case had 13 replies from 428 recipients and 1 contract ([Coldy](https://coldy.ai/en/case-studies/email-outreach-for-webstudio)). The closest anecdote to your setup is an Indie Hackers builder who emailed 43 businesses with their own audit tool and got 17 replies and 1 paid client at $230 per month (search snippet only, original not opened, [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-sent-43-cold-emails-with-my-own-tool-17-replied-1-paid-heres-the-unofficial-launch-329774e623)). Treat the 25-35% vendor claims for hyper-local outreach as outliers.

For job outreach, the best-documented case is a single student who sent 100 personalized emails and got 11 replies (11.5% of delivered), 4 positive, and 2 internship offers ([Whali](https://whali.com/blog/sent-100-cold-emails-internship-results)). That article's breakdowns are unverified, but they agree with the other sources in direction: smaller companies replied more (15% under 30 employees against 4% at 100 or more), a founder recipient beat HR (14% against 2%), under 100 words beat over 200, and a specific insight beat a list of qualifications by about 2x. My planning range is **5-15% replies and 1-3% converting to a call or interview** for hand-reviewed, audit-led emails to small-company owners, and the same email at 1-3% for cold local businesses. These are inferences, not measurements. Target the founder at companies under 100 people, or the head of marketing or growth, and the hiring manager a level or two above the role at larger ones. Send three or four touches over about two weeks, and leave out the CV on the first email.

Be honest with yourself about what is missing. No source found a verified case of a web, SEO or CRO seeker landing a role from a pre-posting signal plus proof of work. Nothing separates freelancer-to-client reply rates for this niche from generic B2B. Nothing measures how recipients react to obviously AI-drafted outreach, and one HN commenter notes proof of work is now easy to fake ([Hacker News](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/46684815)). That makes hand review of every draft part of the strategy, not a nice-to-have. A real, checkable finding, such as a specific failing metric or a broken form, is the thing that holds up.

## Conclusion

The main change from your starting position is the unit of work. Your scanner was built to find websites with problems. The research suggests the careers watcher is most valuable as one more input to that same scanner, where a long-open posting is a pain signal and the audit is the proof of work, not as a separate product that races LinkedIn by a few unmeasured hours. That also resolves the full-time versus contract split. A single email to an owner who is hiring for a role they have not filled can open either door, and you decide which after they reply.

Three things would change this advice. If your measured lead over LinkedIn turns out to be days, not hours, the watcher moves up in priority as a standalone feature. If your first 200-300 emails show audit-led outreach performing near the generic 3.43% benchmark, the audit is not the edge and warm intros and agency partnerships deserve the time. If a compliance deadline such as ADA Title II drives replies from public-sector buyers, that niche may outperform every signal-based idea here, because it offers a legal reason to act in 2027-2028. Run the small tests, record the numbers, and let your own data replace the vendor figures this report had to rely on.
